Game development

Tank Game

A mobile top-down strategy game that I am developing. In the game, you defend a central point from waves of enemies. You can use a wide variety of tanks to defend yourself and air strikes to support you against growing waves of enemies. This forces the player to make tactical decisions and difficult choices to survive the battles. The player starts in an easy mode that starts slowly and gradually builds up the number and difficulty of enemies. This game contains an upgrade menu where the player can buy new tanks and upgrade current ones. When both additional tanks are bought, the option for medium mode unlocks. This mode increases enemy variety and difficulty but gives the player more money in return.

Tanks

In the game, there are five different types of tanks, each with their own strengths and weaknesses. The player can use these tanks to defend against waves of enemies. The player can also use air strikes to support them in battle. The player starts with a limited amount of money and must earn more beating the round. The player can use this money to buy tank cards. The player can also unlock new tanks or upgrade existing ones.

Regular tanks

Regular tanks are good at swarming large enemies. They are cheap and can also serve as distractions. These tanks are the best value, but are vulnerable to attacks that can hit multiple tanks.

Snipers

Snipers are effective at destroying swarms and bunched-together enemies, as their projectiles have a high damage output and can pierce through multiple targets. However, they are vulnerable to close-range attacks and shoot slowly.

Titans

The Titan tank is powerful, fast, and extremely durable. It is designed to take hits from high-damage attacks and close the distance on long-range targets. Unfortunately, it is vulnerable to rapid-fire attacks and swarms.

Cascade

The Cascade tank has by far the longest range and highest damage output. It is effective against grouped enemies due to its ability to pierce through multiple targets at once. This tank has the longest reload time, making it vulnerable when surrounded or facing durable enemies.

Eviscerator

The Eviscerator tank excels at taking down heavily armoured enemies with its rapid-fire weaponry, but it takes additional damage from high-damage weapons, making it an ideal target for snipers and the Cascade. This tank also explodes on death, destroying friends and foes.

Despoiler

The Despoiler tank is a massive enemy boss unit. It has multiple destructible sections, forcing the player to target specific parts to defeat it. It shoots every projectile in the game from its main cannon. The additional weapon attachments on its sides and back have their own durability. It releases a huge explosion on death.

Bombers and Artillery

In the game, the player has access to three artillery strikes, or bombs. These can be used to devastating effect. A meter fills up, and once it completes, a card is placed in your hand.

Empty Void

During most of my free time, I enjoy developing various projects, most of which are games. In 2023, I created a game called Empty Void and uploaded it to my GitHub. This is a game where the player is a small triangle and dodges a wide variety of attacks. These include balls that come at you at wild angles, tracking projectiles, and a wall of distorted shapes that the player has to jump between. The attacks get harder over time and the player is rewarded the longer they survive. You can watch me play my game here.

Later in 2024, my interest in this particular game peaked as I added advertisements to it, which led me to continue working on it. Soon after, I wanted to upload the game to the Google Play Store. I went through a process to find stakeholders, and over a number of weeks I fixed many issues and developed a better game with their advice. Eventually, I uploaded it to Google Play, where it could now be downloaded by anyone (later removed). The game is also able to run advertisements using Unity Ads. This does not make me money, but I wanted to practice implementing third-party features.

In addition to this, I rolled out a few updates, fixing bugs and adding new features. This helped me practice creating working releases without bugs. It may take days to deploy to the Play Store, so each release must be well tested and bug-free to prevent me from rolling back changes or having my app down. Eventually, I decided to remove it from the Play Store, as keeping up with all the security version updates I had to do due to Unity was too much effort.

Programming Project

For my computer science A-level, I made a game. This project took eight months and I documented it. It is a 3D first-person shooter using Unity and affiliated assets. In the project, I used a few assets from the Unity Asset Store, but all the pixel art was created by me. In the game, you travel through three different levels with unique terrains and different enemies, ending in a final fight against a powerful enemy using all the skills learned in the previous levels. The first level introduces basic enemies and healing pickups. The second level adds a more interesting environment, and as I experimented further with lighting and effects. The third and final level is the culmination of all my work, with additional enemies, large and complex arenas, multiple continuous waves of enemies, and a final boss fight that incorporates many different attacks and tests the player's skill.
